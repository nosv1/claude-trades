        Trend and value

        Two simple ways to spot what might keep going up. Trend: things that went up over the past year often keep going up for a while. Value: things much cheaper than their usual price (their average over the last 5 years) often bounce back someday. Both rules have worked on average for decades. But they often fail for any one thing, and they often disagree.

        This is not a shopping list. Everyone can see these same numbers, so prices already reflect them. Use this to notice things, not to pick the perfect time to buy. Funds that track these prices often do worse than the price itself over time. A broad stock fund already owns companies that make and use all of these.

          Metals

          People buy gold when they're worried. Silver is half that and half a factory metal (it's in solar panels and electronics), so its price swings more. Copper goes into wires, buildings and electric cars, so its price shows roughly how busy the world's factories are.

          Food

          The FAO Food Price Index tracks what a basket of food sold between countries costs. FAO is the UN's food agency. 100 means the same as the 2014-2016 average. Wheat, maize (corn), rice and soybeans feed most people, either as food or as feed for farm animals.

          Energy and fertilizer

          Growing and moving food takes a lot of energy. Diesel fuel runs tractors, trucks and ships. Fertilizer (like urea) is made from natural gas. When oil and fertilizer get expensive, food usually gets more expensive a few months later. For example, from 2021 to 2022 gas prices soared and Russia invaded Ukraine. Fertilizer ended up costing 3.5 times as much, and world food prices hit their all-time high.

          Currencies

          Oil, grain and metals are priced in US dollars. When the dollar gets stronger, they cost more for everyone else, and buyers pull back. That pushes their dollar prices down, but it's a push, not a rule. Other countries' money matters too when they sell or buy a lot. The other four charts show how many reals, rubles, yuan or rupees one dollar buys, so higher means that money is weaker.

          When the dollar moved a lot

          From 2007 to 2025, the dollar jumped 8% or more in a year in 2008-09, for a month in 2012, and in 2014-16. Each time oil and world food prices fell, oil by as much as half. In 2022 it jumped again, but oil and food rose anyway, because Russia's war on Ukraine pushed harder. In those same years, when the dollar fell 8% or more, oil rose every time and food in all but one month. Some of this is the same events, like a slowdown, moving both.

          Who feeds whom

          A few countries grow most of the food that gets sold from one country to another. Pick a crop to see who sells it and who buys it. Then tap a seller to see who would be hurt most if it stopped selling. That really happened in 2022, when Russia and Ukraine's grain got cut off.
