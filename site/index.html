    Trend and value

    Two ways to look for what might keep going up. Trend: things that rose over the past year tend to keep rising for a while. Value: things far below their 5-year average tend to recover eventually. Both hold up over decades across many markets, but they're weak for any single item, and they often disagree.

    Not a buy list. Public information like this is already in the price, so treat it as a way to notice things, not to time them. If you invest, most of these trade through ETFs, and commodity funds can lag the price they track over time. Broad stock index funds already own the companies that produce and use them.

      Metals

      Silver is half money, half industrial metal (solar panels, electronics), so it swings harder than gold. Copper goes into wiring, buildings and EVs, which makes it a rough gauge of how busy the world's factories are.

      Food

      The FAO Food Price Index is the world's grocery bill: what a basket of internationally traded food costs, compared with 2014-2016 (= 100). Wheat, maize, rice and soybeans feed most people, either directly or as animal feed.

      What drives food prices

      Food is made from energy. Diesel runs the tractors and ships, and natural gas is the raw material for nitrogen fertilizer like urea. When oil and fertilizer spike, food usually follows a few months later. From early 2021 to spring 2022, as natural gas prices soared and then Russia invaded Ukraine, urea went up about 3.5 times and the food index hit its all-time record.

      Who feeds whom

      A handful of countries grow most of the food the world trades. Pick a crop to see who sells it and who relies on them. Then pick a seller to see who would be hit hardest if it stopped exporting, the way Russia and Ukraine's grain was cut off in 2022.
